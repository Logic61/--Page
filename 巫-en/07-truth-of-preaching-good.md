Many say religion guides people toward good and raises their moral level. But when China had no religion of old, was the Chinese moral level not higher? With religion, doing good is mostly no longer from the heart. Such good springs not from civility and nature but from superstition and greed — how is it better than before?

The ancient Confucian shi culture held: the shi may be killed, not disgraced; the shi dies for one who knows him; death to achieve benevolence, life forfeited for righteousness. The Mohists played the knight-errant, minding the world's injustices — whether from culture or from ritual law. Set beside that, what sort of thing is "saving a life beats building a seven-tiered pagoda"? It turns morality into a vulgar profit. Religious preaching of good is threat and bribe — not even benevolence, much less ritual, much less simplicity. The Hu religion's retribution teaching has in fact badly corrupted the Chinese national character — from brave and firm, loving benevolence and prizing righteousness, gradually toward cowardice and toward weighing gain.

Some may think preaching good by karmic retribution is fine too; but that is no different from midwifing a devil in the heart and then having the devil restrain itself. Objectively, advocating good deeds is a good — but this way morality decays further, in a vicious cycle. The gentleman understands righteousness; the petty man understands profit. Making people do good for profit rather than virtue is itself advocating profit in place of righteousness. Take profit as root, and righteousness is forgotten.

Once a man is used to profit in place of right and wrong, he loses every bottom line; what remains is only the size of the gain or loss — that is, the force of the restraint. Then, if someone tells him believing in Three-Eggs □□□□□□□□□ yields a hundredfold, he at once becomes a Three-Eggs adherent. This is what "Huangdi first troubled men's hearts with benevolence and righteousness" is getting at. When the great Dao declined, benevolence and righteousness appeared; when wisdom came forth, great hypocrisy came. By the stage of religion, even benevolence, righteousness, and ritual are gone — pure threat and bribe. Man loses simplicity; benevolence and righteousness were already unreliable — how much less profit and loss. Thus worse at every step, drawing hearts down into the abyss. So when the Po-men sect speaks of the □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□ tenth incarnation, it is not wholly without sense.

Take Europe now, cold in human feeling: the EU decrees 10 euros for a good deed done, a 10-euro fine for a bad one. Ten years in, cancel the policy — do you think people will still do good? Or will they do bad out of spite? Suppose now Yamo Ruiken puts up the money: 20 euros for each bad deed — would Europe go mad for it? So much for profit in place of morality: the poison runs ten thousand ages, harm without end — not to be compared with the momentary calming of society. So the truly pious Buddhist has no bottom line; it is a matter of principle. Many think Hu-religion thought can settle society and make people good; from the Daoist vantage I hold, rather, that it confuses the people's hearts. The view may be somewhat partial — but stretch out the timespan and see the Buddha-dharma's bad influence on our Hua-xia national character, and you may know it is roughly right.

Do not speak to me of karmic retribution — there is no fixed cause and effect to begin with. The Hu religion's karma teaching reasons from the fruit backward: from a good fruit it deduces a good cause; and where a good cause has a bad fruit, karma, rebirth, and the rest explain it. □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□, mere jugglery.

Religion of course has a popular base; but mostly the crowd is not the right direction. Built on threat and bribe, how can true good arise? At most it is an expedient.

Preach good by threat and people learn cowardice. Preach good by bribe and people learn to chase gain.

Unjust means can hardly reach just ends. The Buddhist's good is only weighing costs: doing good wins rebirth in the pure land and escape from hell — because in their eyes the profit of the pure land is large enough.

Preach good by fear and bribe, and the man who does good today from fear and greed will do evil tomorrow for greater fear and greater greed; in his heart there is only profit, no morality. Such preaching does objectively steady society — and objectively slides its morals further.

Mostly this preaching is to anesthetize the adherents — a spiritual placebo. Letting you accept poverty, accept humiliation, accept exploitation, and repose your hope in heaven and the next life. In religious regions — South India, the Philippines, some African countries — the people have no room or space left for thought. They live in misery, and religion brings them false hope: this is religion's use.

And China's leading the world for millennia is because religion never took the advantage with us; our thought was never imprisoned. And because our common people lived relatively better, religion was less needed. Later the Tungusic-Tatar grip on Han thought, the devastating blow to Han civilization — only then did these religious superstitions overrun. Even so, the Han character, from brave and decisive, life-forfeiting for righteousness in Qin and Han times, slowly turned fearing death, swallowing insult to live on, again and again ruled by aliens. Fortunately the seed-fire did not die; the day of revival could come.

Threat and lure are worse than understanding principles and keeping ritual; the thought of fortune and woe, reward and punishment, corrupts social feeling in the end; it saves trouble but forms a vicious cycle.

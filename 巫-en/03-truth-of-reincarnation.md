The matter of reincarnation may be said not to exist. If all living beings came by reincarnation, who was reborn as the universe's first souled creature? The ancients said it clearly: a man's death is like a lamp put out. Spirit and soul gradually scatter into qi, returning to Heaven, Earth, and nature.

Many people can cite many so-called cases of reincarnation, but they are basically all faked by religions and by those with ulterior motives. Much of it you do not understand; when others advertise it, sworn and solemn, looking as if the evidence were complete, you follow into superstition. Many of these are cases people mistake for reincarnation.

For instance, sometimes a person suddenly gains memories he did not have, or can suddenly do what he could not, or suddenly understands what he did not. Some take this for awakened past-life memories, when it is only some information fragments absorbed — in the ancients' phrase, remnant souls and soul fragments. Some bump into residual information ancients left between Heaven and Earth, or, as it is said, absorb wandering soul fragments — the Daoist term is remnant souls and soul fragments — and so gain memories, and then presume they have discovered their own former life. This "bumping into" is in fact being drawn by the person, and in its dimness seeking to possess him; but such remnant souls are very weak, and so are absorbed by the person. Some receive remnants left by different ancients or even modern people and take them for different former lives — having absorbed a wisp of remnant information □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□, they truly have no former lives or rebirth.

This absorbing of remnant souls and soul fragments is extremely common; almost everyone meets it, and every life passes through it many times. In infancy, even in youth, people often fall ill once and thereby learn many things on their own, even change greatly in temperament and habits — have you never wondered why? Some children run a fever for no cause, or fall into a dazed illness; when it passes, their speech is glib, they no longer wet the bed, they can count, and so on. The old saying that children learn things in dreams has sense to it. Once grown, bumping into remnant souls and soul fragments again, a person finds those simple bits of knowledge long since learned rarely yield any gain, and may even go unnoticed. But not wholly absent — some gain more, some less. Some, for example, learn in a dream the pacing of the Dipper and the striding of Gang — perhaps having absorbed a Daoist's remnant soul; of course yin-master possession-transmission is not excluded either. And of course you may think this explanation too plain, not awesome enough, no sense of superiority. Then you may call it primal-spirit deduction: your primal spirit, drawing on all the information stored in your brain, deducing the future, guessing from present elements at countless things that might occur. What the primal spirit deduces in dreams has some chance of happening; when it happens, your residual impression of the dream and the bits you remember may make you mistake it for a past-life memory.

Another case is choosing one's own womb. Most people after death are muddled, much like fools; unless delivered to a dharma-realm, or meeting such special circumstances as a yin-nourishing ground, they will basically scatter away.

But a very few tougher souls can take a womb on their own — mostly cultivators. Their cultivation failed, yet they keep a measure of awareness and choose a womb to be reborn. If stronger and luckier, they keep some memories and habits as well.

This is rebirth, but not reincarnation. Those who truly choose their own rebirth and return by vow — whatever the memories, they recall it within a few years of age; if they cannot recall within a few years, it will not come at all; cultivation might recover a little, never much. So when an adult suddenly gains memories, it is yin-transmission, soul fragments, possession. Of the knowledge known while possessed, some is remembered afterward; some is lost when the possessing thing departs.

Yet reincarnation cannot be said not to exist at all. Only, reincarnation is no native law of the universe, nor a universal thing, but merely a crippled system within certain religions. This reincarnation system is laid out by doctrine, formed from the incense-fire thought-power of thousands of believers — a mechanism of the dharma-realm, one might say a split-spirit or portion of that religion's deity-lord. And it is not as advertised: even walking its system, so-called former and future lives are in fact none of your affair. Some religious adherents after death follow the link of incense-fire thought to the religious dharma-realm; some without faith are dragged there in deliverance. These people enjoy 007 blessings in the dharma-realm, glowing and giving heat as batteries; when the yin-lifespan ends, or the soul becomes jian and unusable, they follow the religion's reincarnation system to be reborn. This reincarnation also follows the incense-fire link to a womb, so the object can only be a female adherent of the same religion. Some divine-way cultivators choosing their own rebirth take this channel too.

So: if your mother is not an adherent of some religion with a doctrine of reincarnation, you can basically rule out having come by way of the reincarnation system.

A person of no faith and no cultivation does have a chance at this reincarnation channel. The ancients said, □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□. To reach a dharma-realm you do not need a lifetime's faith — the piety of the dying moment suffices. As certain religions say, call such-and-such a name at death and be saved. A faith-link struck in an instant can pull one to a dharma-realm all the same, □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□.

Reincarnation thought came in with the Hu religion; China had no such teaching of old. But now many are assimilated by Hu-religion culture, □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□. Brainwashed by Hu-religion thought, people hold mistaken notions of reincarnation, all kinds of hopes. All day long "this life and the next," "last life and the life after," thinking it is their soul continuing.

But this so-called reincarnation is utterly meaningless, for a soul that goes through the reincarnation channel — memory and soul alike — has been ground away nine parts and nine-nines, on the verge of scattering. Even if a smidgeon of remnant information recalls a former life, it is like watching a heap of video fragments — not even as immersive as a film.

In a sense, this old motorcycle of yours no longer exists. What exists is a new vehicle fitted with a few refurbished parts.

So the living: either live well and work well, putting hope in this life; or cultivate in earnest and strive to save themselves. If old and unaccomplished — then let a man's death be a lamp put out. Bones ground to ash, soul scattered — meeting death and life with open calm is itself a kind of breadth.

A religious dharma-realm admits only its own believers. Those who truly succeed in cultivation □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□. They will not even bother with the human world — how much less a dharma-realm?

We said earlier that gods are all man-made. If gods are man-made, where do heaven and hell, the celestial court and the underworld bureaus, the six paths of reincarnation, the Buddha-lands and pure lands — all these dharma-realms — come from? The dharma-realm lies within the spirit world — the world of consciousness, the layer of the world where the consciousness of every living being resides; part of our universe. A dharma-realm is the half-enclosed territory of a religion or of gods and ghosts within the spirit world — like different nations on Earth. Beyond the dharma-realms lies the free zone. □□□□□□□□□□ the dharma-realms of various teachings and those of other gods and ghosts are all parts of the spirit world — like staking out pasture in the spirit world. The underworld besides is a place in the spirit world naturally suited to yin-spirits to live — now with a master, now without — of half-dharma-realm character. It is said true men, penetrating the mystery of being and non-being, can make all dharmas mind-only, and create worlds and open realms. Here it is much the same principle, but a crude Paleolithic hand-made version. Countless believers' faith constitutes these spaces — only they are not actual realms. A dharma-realm is either an empty realm or dwells between the empty and the actual, lodged upon the material universe. For the realm of the gods is not yet that high: they cannot break out of the believers' bonds, cannot break out of Heaven and Earth's bonds; they cannot achieve the mutual arising of empty and actual, much less the mutual transformation of being and non-being.

Simply put: a man's death should be like a lamp put out, but countless people believe in an underworld, a heaven, a hell, a reincarnation — and after death, by their own inner judgment of themselves, they go to these places. Then by the religious system they believed in, either the soul is straight away collected by the god, or it persists a while in the dharma-realm until the yin-lifespan ends, the spirit scatters, and a wisp of spirit-light turns to rebirth. These scattered spirits are also absorbed by the gods and the dharma-realm. Only sustained by countless people's faith can such a state exist, letting dead souls live in it a while — but not endlessly; one day it too scatters or turns into reincarnation. So are those who believe in no god, no religion, left only to have their souls scattered? Not entirely. Some disbelieve in gods yet inwardly hold that an underworld exists — after death they go there. Some, in special circumstances, nourished by yin-qi, become ghosts.

In monotheistic systems, or systems with a deity-lord, every being of the dharma-realm is a split-spirit of the realm's lord — from all the servant-gods to the angels, heavenly kings, heavenly masters, and the heavenly soldiers, generals, and horses: no exception, even their enemies. □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□. In egalitarian polytheisms, their subordinate gods and servants are likewise their split-spirits, belonging to the gods themselves, but the equal gods divide and share the dharma-realm among them.

In the Daoist school, the sayings of ghost, jian, xi, yi, wei describe the process by which a person after death gradually scatters into qi.

Xi, yi, wei — unseen, unheard, untouchable: these in fact speak of qi, with no individual reference. Ghost and jian, however, are spirit-bodies with concrete reference. In the dharma-realm, the souls of adherents absorb yin-sha and the cruder incense-fire, and produce comparatively refined thought-power. The mixed qi of incense-fire, filtered once through the believer's soul, can do as little harm to the deity-lord as possible. When a believer's soul is polluted beyond use, it turns from ghost to jian, cast out of the dharma-realm to fend for itself — or tossed into the reincarnation system formed of faith-power, to be reborn in a female believer's belly. What believers do in a dharma-realm is in fact serve as batteries and filters. A dead adherent is a good adherent; live adherents mostly give polluted thought-power, bringing the deity-lord only trouble, only degeneration. So religions generally do not wish their adherents long life. Yet without adherents in the human world, where do dharma-realm batteries come from? The problem has no solution — gods and Buddhas cannot make souls — so they forever sit at the edge of degeneration, wildly testing the line. The great religions generally oppose suicide because the soul of a suicide is a defective product, hard to use. □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□.

Some cultivated adherents sell hot after death, fought over by the deity-lord's "customers", with better treatment in the dharma-realm. Such souls are of higher quality, produce more thought-power, and have a longer service term — one section outlasting six sections. And the deity-lord intercepts part of the thought-power they contribute to strengthen their soul-body and craft more perfect pure-land bliss illusions — so their service life grows longer still. If the adherent had some attainment in cultivation, then they are basically already a split-spirit of the deity-lord. Such split-spirits used merely as batteries would be great talent wasted. So generally the deity-lord will not straight away collect them, or use them only as batteries, but keep some self-awareness in them, letting them keep cultivating and keep offering thought-power. And may, by their attainment, grant them an office — as divine servant or servant-god, handling affairs for the deity-lord. Dharma-realm civil servants! Other dharma-realm administrators are clergy who earned merit by preaching; by the merit of enlarging the faith and growing incense-fire for the deity-lord, they can become middle- and lower-level managers of the dharma-realm.

Besides the large dharma-realms, there are small ones, leaning near god-statues or temples: a temple too has its spirit-world territory, where nearby clergy and congregants may lean long- or short-term. Take deliverance rites: some deliver the dead directly into a dharma-realm; some wholly faithless ghosts are first delivered to the temple, then transferred to the dharma-realm. □□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□□. These small gods' or temples' own dharma-realm is small, but radiates outward with the temple's incense-fire, up to the range their divine office covers — this range is called the dharma-domain. These small gods are their complete selves within the dharma-realm, and within the dharma-domain also have the support of office and incense-fire; outside the domain, their power decays badly.

If a dharma-realm loses its source of believers, it can only recruit wandering ghosts and faithless cultivators in the spirit world to sustain itself, and may gradually decline and even collapse. God and dharma-realm are one and indivisible — honor shared, ruin shared.

On spells: many are interested, few know how they really work. In principle, spells come down in the end to spirit-qi.

First, self-power spells require some cultivation stage. The difference between qi-spells and spirit-spells is that one is done by spirit, one by qi. Spirit's reach is larger, qi's much smaller; but some simple spells — moving objects at a distance, striking an ox through a mountain, binding a person, killing or healing — qi can do. Such spells are in fact extremely simple; once the inner qi reaches, they are there. Past the prenatal stage, with deeper standing, qi naturally can be sent out; postnatal qi has sending-out methods too, but through hard training; martial arts have sending-out methods as well — which I do not know.

What qi can do, spirit at a certain level can do too; what spirit can do, qi cannot always. Spirit-done spells are, early on, mostly nature spells — water, fire, wind, rain, thunder — of varying difficulty. Wind spells, water spells, or killing and confusing people are easiest. Not much qi is needed; mostly four ounces shifting a thousand catties.

Those of you who have read much spell material know: the chief principle of spells is the inner spirit communicating with the outer spirit.

What is the inner spirit? Your soul and spirit. When they are cultivated deep enough, one can attempt the sense of Heaven-and-man as one, and feel the outer spirit. What is the outer spirit? The Dao moves through all things; all things have their virtue. The outer spirit is the virtue of all things, the nature of all things — the spirit of wind, say, the spirit of water.

When inner spirit reaches outer spirit, a spell can form, four ounces to a thousand catties. Raising a breeze, gathering thin mist — the simplest. As for fire and thunder, strongly yang, the yin-spirit cannot; only when yin-spirit or ghost-immortal sheds its yin-nature and becomes primal spirit or yang-spirit can it be done. And spirit-driving-things, a muscular job, depends on the condensation of one's spirit.

But whatever the spell, spirit or qi, even at four ounces to a thousand catties, it drains one hugely. Even cultivating daily, one cannot afford endless waste. Some qi of certain natures can be refined and recycled — yin-qi, thunder-qi, prenatal qi. Some, like wind and water, solid things, can only be influenced indirectly. Outer spirit denotes a nature only, not a spirit-and-soul. With true soul, not just gathered faith-force, it would be a true prenatal nature deity — certain mountain gods and water gods.

Transport methods, mirror-circle arts and the like all communicate with ghosts and gods. These are divine-way spells.

Here too the inner spirit links an outer spirit — but the outer spirit is a ghost or god. Another kind: your soul contacts an accomplished ancestor; by the ancestor's contract with other ghosts and gods, he dispatches them to work the spell, producing the effect. The ancestor may be an immortal, a deity, or a yin-spirit.

Of those who emit qi to heal: some is ability trained up; some is divine-way spell — hard to tell apart from looks. Emitted qi tugs the patient's qi-movements, sinews, muscles; the patient may move spontaneously. The ghost-communication sort enters by possession, tugs the qi-movements, and the body moves wildly — jerking about like one bewitched; this is clearly no spontaneous movement but more likely possession.

Such spells are far simpler; many simple ones work after merely passing the method or joining the school. Spells can also attain the way. How? Spell practice can accomplish yin-spirit, ghost-immortal, even primal spirit — provided it is self-cultivated. Divine-way spells, even cultivated to the peak, live on the god's breath, □□□□□□□□□□□□□. Some who commune especially well with outer spirits accomplish yin-spirit and ghost-immortal; after death they merge with the outer spirit, possibly becoming nature deities; carrying on further is hard, yet earthly and heavenly immortality remain possible — that road at least is not severed. By spell practice one labors no less than by proper Daoist cultivation, and attaining the way is far harder.

As for divine-way thunder methods: they do cunsi on an enfeoffed thunder god or on talismans. And incantations — sometimes to commune with gods, sometimes just a trigger to gather one's own spirit. God-communication talismans and incantations are in fact an agreement with ghosts and gods, a marker: whom you call, what you bid him do. A spirit-trigger is like shouting "Attention!" — you snap to attention yourself. Shout it at an American and he does not understand.

In fact few thunder-method practitioners are struck dead — most practice divine-way thunder, with at most a thunder-nature that may not even frighten ghosts. The Five-Thunder method is only five-qi application, not true thunder — five-qi thunder. Some thunder methods may be like air-compression cannons, bang-shock "thunder". Even heaven-thunder practice goes step by step; knowing death is easy, who goes seeking it?

Spells, frankly, are of no great use. Take transport arts: have you no hands? Would hands not move it faster? One may say most spell-men learn spells from crooked hearts — those wanting transport, wall-passing, x-ray sight are teachers of lewdness and brigandage, thinking to peep at women bathing, to move others' goods, or to make money by it.

Spells are not mysterious at all. Nature spells so; the rest likewise. Forms vary, the principle stands: Buddhist, Daoist, shaman, priest — the reasoning is this reasoning.
